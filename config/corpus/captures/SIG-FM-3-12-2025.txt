FM 3-12 CYBERSPACE AND ELECTROMAGNETIC WARFARE OPERATIONS SEPTEMBER 2025 DISTRIBUTION RESTRICTION: Approved for public release; distribution is unlimited. This publication supersedes FM 3-12, dated 24 August 2021. HEADQUARTERS, DEPARTMENT OF THE ARMY Foreword Over the past several years, the global security environment has changed in ways once considered unthinkable in the post-Cold War world order. Where the Department of Defense once believed the future of warfare was antiterrorism and limited contingency operations, it is now clear that the prospect of large-scale combat against a regional peer is much more likely than once believed. In recent years, Russia has engaged in the largest land war in Europe of the past 80 years. China, with an economy nearly equal in size to ours, is fostering new international partnerships through its Belt and Road Initiative and building a world -class military to challenge the United States, its allies, and neighbors in the Pacific Rim, including in Taiwan. China and Russia, alon g with other regional peers, have heavily invested in and demonstrated formidable capabilities to challenge U.S. dominance in multiple domains, including cyberspace and the electromagnetic spectrum. The Army’s operating concept of multidomain operations anticipates the return of great power competition and large-scale combat operations and stresses the need to integrate and converge warfighting capabilities in all domains to achieve and press position s of relative advantage. At the same time, the Army’s newest doctrine for information presents a new paradigm for how the Army views information in a military context. Data and information are central to command and control, for our adversaries as much as for ourselves. Advances in sensors, networking technologies, and global connectivity provide commanders an unprecedented amount of data and information to support informed decision making. These capabilities are almost wholly dependent on cyberspace and the electromagnetic spectrum. Peer threats continually attempt to infiltrate, exploit, and degrade access to and ability to use our networks and data. It is therefore vital that the Army not only protect its own information and cyberspace capabilities, but also deny our adversaries the use of similar capabilities. Creating effects in cyberspace and the electromagnetic spectrum across the competition continuum is key to achieving relative advantages. To maximize these advantages, commanders must integrate and synchronize cyberspace operations and electromagnetic warf are with all other available military capabilities through a combined arms approach.
